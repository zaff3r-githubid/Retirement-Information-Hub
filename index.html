Five questions to ask

Retirement isn't one decision — it's five overlapping ones. Knowing which question you're asking prevents conflating them.

1

When to stop working

There is no single U.S. retirement age. Work, health, savings, and employer benefits drive this personal date.

2

When to claim benefits

Social Security can start at 62, full retirement age is 66–67 by birth year, and delaying increases benefits up to age 70.

3

How to cover healthcare

Medicare typically begins at 65, but enrollment timing and employer coverage can change what makes sense.

4

How to draw income

401(k), IRA, pension, Social Security, and part-time work each have different tax and flexibility tradeoffs.

5

How to protect the family

Spousal benefits, survivor benefits, beneficiary forms, powers of attorney, and fraud prevention can matter as much as account balances.

Milestones

Retirement ages worth knowing

Key federal age thresholds that affect benefits, penalties, and account rules.

50

Catch-up contributions may begin

Many retirement accounts allow higher annual contribution limits starting at 50. Limits change year to year — verify with current IRS guidance before maximizing.

59½

Early withdrawal penalty generally ends

The common 10% additional IRS tax on early distributions no longer applies after this age. Withdrawals are still taxable income — only the penalty expires.

62

Earliest Social Security retirement claim

Claiming at 62 creates earlier income, but monthly benefits are permanently reduced compared with claiming at full retirement age.

65

Medicare eligibility begins

Most people first become eligible for Medicare at 65. The initial enrollment window opens before the birthday month. Missing it can trigger lasting premium penalties.

66–67

Full retirement age (FRA)

Social Security FRA depends on birth year — it reaches 67 for those born in 1960 or later. At FRA, you receive 100% of your calculated benefit with no permanent reduction.

70

Delayed Social Security credits stop

Waiting past FRA earns delayed retirement credits (roughly 8% per year). These credits stop accruing at 70, so there is no benefit to delaying beyond that age.

73+

Required Minimum Distributions

RMD start ages depend on birth year and account type. Missing a required withdrawal can trigger IRS penalties — verify the rules before your first required year.

Social Security

When to claim Social Security

Benefits can begin as early as 62 with sufficient work history. Full retirement age is 67 for those born in 1960 or later. Delaying past FRA earns credits that stop at 70.

SSA retirement benefits

Reasons to claim earlier

  • Creates income sooner if work stops, health changes, or cash flow is tight
  • May reduce early pressure on savings
  • Can make sense when life expectancy or caregiving needs point that way

Tradeoffs of claiming earlier

  • Monthly benefit is permanently reduced before full retirement age
  • Working while under FRA can temporarily reduce current benefits
  • Lower monthly income matters more if one spouse significantly outlives the other

Family benefits

Spousal and survivor benefits

Social Security extends to spouses, divorced spouses, and survivors. When one spouse earned significantly more or paused work for caregiving, checking both records can reveal benefits that might otherwise be missed.

401(k), IRA, pensions

Retirement account withdrawals

IRS guidance says 401(k) distributions are generally taxable income. Withdrawals before age 59½ may also face a 10% additional tax unless a specific exception applies.

IRS 401(k) distribution rules

Advantages

Tax-advantaged growth, possible employer match, automatic payroll contributions, and broad investment options over time.

Tradeoffs

Withdrawals raise taxable income, early access can be penalized, and market conditions affect account value at distribution.

Questions to verify

Rollover options, RMD timing, Roth vs. traditional tax treatment, beneficiary designations, employer plan rules, and state taxes.

Required Minimum Distributions (RMDs): Most traditional retirement accounts require annual withdrawals beginning at age 73 (shifting to 75 in 2033 under current law). Missed RMDs can trigger significant IRS penalties. IRS RMD FAQs

Healthcare

Medicare coverage choices

Medicare is the main federal health program for people 65 and older. The two main tracks have different cost structures, provider access, and flexibility tradeoffs.

Medicare.gov: Coverage choices
Feature Original Medicare
Part A + B (+ Part D, Medigap)		 Medicare Advantage
Part C — private plan
Provider access Broad — any doctor who accepts Medicare Usually limited to plan network
Out-of-pocket cap No built-in cap without Medigap Yes — annual limit on covered services
Drug coverage Separate Part D plan required Often bundled in plan
Dental / vision / hearing Not covered by Original Medicare Some plans include extras
Switching later Flexible; Medigap may need underwriting if added later Plans change annually; returning to Medigap may involve underwriting
Coverage gap: Original Medicare does not cover most long-term care, routine dental, routine vision, or hearing aids. Plan for those separately. Medicare.gov: What's not covered

Also worth discussing

Other retirement planning topics

Income taxes

Social Security, pension, IRA, and 401(k) income can be taxed differently. Check federal and state treatment before choosing a drawdown order.

Long-term care

Medicare has limited long-term care coverage. Family caregiving, Medicaid eligibility, and home modifications deserve early discussion.

Housing & home equity

Reverse mortgages are for homeowners 62+, but CFPB warns balances grow and obligations for taxes, insurance, and upkeep remain.

Estate basics

Beneficiary forms, wills, powers of attorney, and healthcare proxies can prevent family confusion during stressful moments.

Fraud prevention

Retirees are frequent scam targets. Treat urgent calls, gift-card requests, and guaranteed-return investments as red flags.

Over-50 benefits

National Parks Senior Pass (62+), AARP membership (50+), senior tax deductions (IRS), and local services via Eldercare Locator.
